\paragraph{Input Preprocessing} Every volume is reoriented to the frame of the backbone's pretraining and resampled to its grid. TAP-CT: LPS, native slice spacing, in-plane trilinear resize to $224\times224$, depth padded with air to at least 12 slices and to a multiple of 4; Hounsfield units (HU) clipped to $[-1008, 822]$ and standardized with the model's mean and standard deviation; windows of 12 slices with a stride of 3 slices plus a final window ending at the last slice (at most 32 windows per training step, all windows at evaluation). SPECTRE: RAS, $0.5\times0.5\times1$\,mm, centered $512\times512\times256$ field of view padded with the scan minimum, HU in $[-1000, 1000]$ scaled to $[0,1]$; a $4\times4\times4$ grid of 64 crops of $128\times128\times64$ voxels at evaluation and a random contiguous $2\times2\times2$ sub-grid of 8 crops at training; each crop is summarized by its CLS token concatenated with its mean patch token before the feature combiner. Pillar-0: LPI, $0.5\times0.5\times1.25$\,mm, centered $128\times256\times256$ volume, eleven input channels (ten fixed center/width windows and a per-volume min--max channel), one forward pass; the linear head reads the model's L2-normalized pooled embedding (three scales concatenated, 1152-d), the attentive head reads the tokens of the three scales, any scale with more than 4096 tokens being average-pooled on its grid. For segmentation, TAP-CT keeps the classification grid (in-plane 224, native depth) and crops of $12\times224\times224$ voxels; SPECTRE resamples to 1\,mm and uses crops of $64\times128\times128$ voxels. A training crop is centered on a random foreground voxel of the run's structures with probability 0.67 and placed uniformly otherwise; volumes smaller than the crop are padded with the image minimum. At evaluation the whole volume is covered by a sliding window with Gaussian weighting (overlap 0.5 for TAP-CT, 0.25 for SPECTRE) and Dice is computed on the backbone's working grid, without post-processing.

\paragraph{Heads} The linear head is dropout (0.2) followed by one linear layer on the pooled feature: the mean of the window CLS tokens for TAP-CT (768-d), the feature combiner's CLS token for SPECTRE (1080-d) and the pooled image embedding for Pillar-0 (1152-d). The attentive probe prepends a learnable CLS token to the token set (the window CLS tokens for TAP-CT, the combiner tokens for SPECTRE and the multi-scale tokens for Pillar-0), applies two pre-norm transformer blocks with eight-head self-attention and a GELU feed-forward layer of ratio 4, normalizes the CLS output and maps it through dropout (0.2) and a linear layer. For one label, the linear head has 769, 1081 and 1153 parameters and the attentive probe 14.2, 28.0 and 31.9\,M on TAP-CT, SPECTRE and Pillar-0, respectively. LoRA+ABMIL pools the window or crop tokens by gated attention \citep{ilse2018abmil}, $a_i=\mathrm{softmax}_i\big(\bm{w}^\top(\tanh(\bm{V}\bm{x}_i)\odot\sigma(\bm{U}\bm{x}_i))\big)$ with hidden size 128, and feeds the pooled token to the attentive probe. The segmentation linear probe is a $1\times1\times1$ convolution on the deepest grid (TAP-CT block 12, SPECTRE block 24), upsampled trilinearly. The decoder projects each of the four grids to 128 channels with a $1\times1\times1$ convolution, resizes them to a common grid (the token grid), concatenates them (512 channels), fuses them with a $3^3$ convolution, instance normalization and leaky ReLU to 128 channels, then applies stages of trilinear $\times2$ upsampling followed by the same convolution block that halves the channels (to a minimum of 16) until the voxel grid is reached (three stages for TAP-CT, four for SPECTRE), and ends with a $1\times1\times1$ convolution to one logit per structure. It has 2.45\,M (TAP-CT) and 2.62\,M (SPECTRE) parameters plus $17C$ for $C$ structures (the final $1\times1\times1$ convolution from 16 channels), and no skip connections from the input.

\paragraph{Adapters} LoRA \citep{hu2022lora} adds $\tfrac{\alpha}{r}\bm{B}\bm{A}\,\mathrm{drop}(\bm{x})$ to the output $\bm{W}_0\bm{x}$ of a frozen projection, with $r=16$, $\alpha=32$ and dropout 0.1; $\bm{A}$ is initialized Kaiming-uniform and $\bm{B}$ at zero, so training starts from the pretrained model. It is applied to the fused query/key/value projection of every TAP-CT block and to the separate query and key/value projections of every SPECTRE block, of SPECTRE's feature combiner, and of every Pillar-0 block. Late adapters cover TAP-CT blocks 9--12, SPECTRE blocks 17--24 and its feature combiner and the third stage of Pillar-0 (8 of 12 blocks). rsLoRA scales the update by $\alpha/\sqrt{r}$ instead of $\alpha/r$. DoRA \citep{liu2024dora} rescales each row of the adapted weight to a learned magnitude $\bm{m}$, $\bm{W}=\bm{m}\odot(\bm{W}_0+\tfrac{\alpha}{r}\bm{B}\bm{A})/\lVert\bm{W}_0+\tfrac{\alpha}{r}\bm{B}\bm{A}\rVert_{\text{row}}$, with $\bm{m}$ initialized to the row norms of $\bm{W}_0$; the magnitude is a two-dimensional parameter and receives weight decay like the adapter matrices. Attention-only fine-tuning unfreezes every parameter of the attention modules that LoRA would adapt (query, key, value and output projections with their biases). On Pillar-0, the per-scale blocks of scales that have already left the encoder and the text projection are also wrapped or unfrozen, but they never run and receive no gradient; counting them, LoRA updates 1.1\,M, LoRA$_{\text{late}}$ 0.74\,M, Attn-only FT 21\,M and Full FT 94\,M parameters (Fig.~\ref{fig:arms}c excludes them). In segmentation the SPECTRE adapters and full fine-tuning also cover the feature combiner, which the segmentation path does not evaluate; those parameters receive no gradient.

\paragraph{Two-stage Methods} Lin$\to$LoRA and Attn$\to$LoRA take the probe weights from the parent run's checkpoint at step 4000 of its 8000-step schedule, add late-block LoRA with $\bm{B}=0$, and train 4000 further steps with a fresh optimizer and schedule. Dec$\to$LoRA takes the decoder from the Dec run at the midpoint of its schedule (step 4000; step 12\,000 for the 24\,000-step joint models) and trains all-block LoRA with the decoder for the second half.

\paragraph{Optimization} We used AdamW with the default $\beta=(0.9, 0.999)$ and $\epsilon=10^{-8}$ in all runs. The learning rate rises linearly from 1\% of its peak over the first 10\% of the steps and then follows a cosine to $10^{-6}$. Weight decay is applied to parameters of dimension two or more, excluding biases and normalization weights. Gradients are not clipped. Classification trains at batch size 1 without gradient accumulation; segmentation at a micro-batch of 2 crops with accumulation 2, so four crops per step. A training step uses $B$ cases $b=1,\dots,B$ ($B=1$ in classification; $B=2$ crops per micro-batch in segmentation).

\emph{Model outputs.} A classification cohort has $K$ binary labels $t=1,\dots,K$. For case $b$, a per-task model for label $t$ outputs one logit $z_{bt}\in\mathbb{R}$, and a joint model outputs one logit per label, $\bm{z}_b=(z_{b1},\dots,z_{bK})$; the reference is $y_{bt}\in\{0,1\}$. A segmentation dataset has $C$ structures $c=1,\dots,C$. For a crop $b$ of $V$ voxels, a per-structure model for structure $c$ outputs one logit map $\bm{z}_{bc}\in\mathbb{R}^{V}$, and a joint model outputs one map per structure, $\bm{z}_{b1},\dots,\bm{z}_{bC}$; the reference is the binary mask $\bm{y}_{bc}\in\{0,1\}^{V}$. Both tasks use the binary cross-entropy of a logit $z$ with reference $y$ and positive weight $w$,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{BCE}(z, y; w)=-\,w\,y\log\sigma(z)-(1-y)\log\big(1-\sigma(z)\big),
\label{eq:bce}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma$ is the logistic sigmoid.

\emph{Classification.} A per-task model for label $t$ is trained only on the cases annotated for label $t$, and a joint model on all cases of the cohort, with the losses
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:lcls}
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}^{\text{per-task}}_{\text{cls}}(t)&=\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\mathrm{BCE}(z_{bt}, y_{bt}; w_t), &
w_t&=\mathrm{clip}\Big(\frac{n^{-}_t}{n^{+}_t},\,0.05,\,20\Big), \label{eq:lcls_pt}\\
\mathcal{L}^{\text{joint}}_{\text{cls}}&=\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\sum_{t=1}^{K} m_{bt}\,\mathrm{BCE}(z_{bt}, y_{bt}; w_t), &
m_{bt}&=\mathbf{1}\big[\,y_{bt}\text{ is annotated}\,\big], \label{eq:lcls_joint}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
where $n^{-}_t$ and $n^{+}_t$ are the numbers of negative and positive training cases of label $t$, and the mask $m_{bt}$ is 1 if label $t$ is annotated for case $b$ and 0 otherwise. Every case is annotated for every label of its cohort ($m_{bt}=1$), except in CVPR26 ($K=17$), where each COVID-CTset and LUNA25 case is annotated for its own label only and each AMOS case for a subset of the 15 AMOS labels.

\emph{Segmentation.} For one structure of one crop, the loss is the soft Dice loss plus the voxel-averaged, unweighted binary cross-entropy,
\begin{align}
\ell(\bm{z}, \bm{y})&=1-\mathrm{Dice}\big(\sigma(\bm{z}), \bm{y}\big)+\frac{1}{V}\sum_{v=1}^{V}\mathrm{BCE}(z_v, y_v; 1), \label{eq:lseg_term}\\
\mathrm{Dice}(\bm{p}, \bm{y})&=\frac{2\sum_{v} p_v y_v+1}{\sum_{v} p_v+\sum_{v} y_v+1}, \notag
\end{align}
where $\mathrm{Dice}$ is the soft Dice coefficient of the voxel probabilities $\bm{p}=\sigma(\bm{z})$ with a smoothing constant of 1. A per-structure model for structure $c$ and a joint model are trained on
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:lseg}
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}^{\text{per-structure}}_{\text{seg}}(c)&=\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\ell(\bm{z}_{bc}, \bm{y}_{bc}), \label{eq:lseg_pt}\\
\mathcal{L}^{\text{joint}}_{\text{seg}}&=\frac{1}{BC}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\sum_{c=1}^{C}\ell(\bm{z}_{bc}, \bm{y}_{bc}). \label{eq:lseg_joint}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
The joint classification loss thus sums over labels, whereas the joint segmentation loss averages over structures. In the joint CVPR26 models the three sources are sampled in the ratio 10:8:8 (AMOS, COVID-CTset, LUNA25); every other run cycles one shuffled training set.

\paragraph{Model Selection and Per-case Outputs} Every 1000 steps the selection and held-out splits are evaluated and a checkpoint of the trained parameters is written; the checkpoint with the highest selection macro AUROC (mean over labels with both classes present) or mean Dice (mean over structures of the mean over cases in which the structure is present in the reference or the prediction) is kept, ties keeping the earlier checkpoint. For TotalSegmentator only the selection split is evaluated during training; the held-out split is evaluated afterwards with the selected checkpoint, and the recomputed selection Dice is required to match the recorded value within 0.005. Per case, the held-out probability of every known label (classification) and the Dice of every structure (segmentation) at the selected checkpoint are stored and are the input of every number in this paper. Dice thresholds the logits at 0; a structure enters a case's mean when the reference or the prediction contains it, so a false-positive structure scores 0 and an absent, unpredicted structure is skipped.

\section{Paired comparisons and further analyses}
\label{app:paired}

Besides the figures and tables cited in the main text, this appendix contains the following analyses.

\paragraph{Ranks of the Classification Methods} To compare the eleven classification methods beyond their mean differences, we ranked them within each of the \numRankSlotsN{} label--mode pairs, that is, each label in its per-task and in its joint model, and report the mean ranks together with the trainable parameters and the AUROC deficit to the best method of each pair in Tab.~\ref{tab:methods}. Attention-only fine-tuning had the best mean rank (\numRankAttnftMean{}) and the smallest mean deficit (\numRegretAttnft{}), and LoRA the second-best rank (\numRankLoraMean{}), whereas the linear probe had the worst rank and the largest deficit (\numRankLinMean{} and \numRegretLin{}).

\paragraph{Per-label Paired Tests} To test the differences label by label rather than on average, we compared every classification method with Lin, LoRA, Attn and Full FT in each label--mode pair with DeLong's test (Tab.~\ref{tab:paired}), and every segmentation method with Lin and the frozen decoder in each structure--mode pair with a paired bootstrap over cases (Tab.~\ref{tab:segpaired}), in both cases over the per-task and joint models and with Benjamini--Hochberg correction. LoRA was ahead of the linear probe in \numPairedTapctLinLoraBetter\%, \numPairedSpectreLinLoraBetter\% and \numPairedPillarZLinLoraBetter\% of the pairs on TAP-CT, SPECTRE and Pillar-0, and significantly behind it in none. The share of significant gains was largest on Pillar-0 (\numPairedPillarZLinLoraSigbetter\%), whose RSNA19 ICH held-out split is the largest (Tab.~\ref{tab:headline}). In segmentation, the frozen decoder was significantly better than the linear probe in \numSegpairedTapctLinDecSigbetter\% (TAP-CT) and \numSegpairedSpectreLinDecSigbetter\% (SPECTRE) of the structure--mode pairs, whereas LoRA on top of the decoder was significantly better in \numSegpairedTapctDecLoraSigbetter\% and \numSegpairedSpectreDecLoraSigbetter\% and significantly worse in \numSegpairedTapctDecLoraSigworse\% and \numSegpairedSpectreDecLoraSigworse\%, respectively.

\paragraph{Encoder Adaptation on Top of the Decoder} To locate where adapting the encoder beneath the decoder helps, Tab.~\ref{tab:segmodes} gives LoRA $-$ Dec and Full FT $-$ Dec per dataset and training mode: per-structure models, joint models and, for the two 15-structure datasets, the joint models under the shared 8000 steps. LoRA $-$ Dec was larger in the joint models ($\numCiSegloradecjtTapctMeandiff$ on TAP-CT and $\numCiSegloradecjtSpectreMeandiff$ on SPECTRE) than in the per-structure models ($\numCiSegloradecptTapctMeandiff$ and $\numCiSegloradecptSpectreMeandiff$; Tab.~\ref{tab:key}). Under the shared 8000 steps, the 15-structure joint LoRA models were behind the decoder on both datasets and backbones; Fig.~\ref{fig:segbudget} follows the selection Dice of the AMOS22 joint models during training. With 24\,000 steps, the AMOS22 joint LoRA model overtook the decoder, its held-out Dice rising from \numSegbudgetAmosBBTapctJointHKLora{} to \numSegbudgetAmosBBTapctJointLora{} on TAP-CT and from \numSegbudgetAmosBBSpectreJointHKLora{} to \numSegbudgetAmosBBSpectreJointLora{} on SPECTRE, against \numSegbudgetAmosBBTapctJointDec{} and \numSegbudgetAmosBBSpectreJointDec{} for the decoder (Tab.~\ref{tab:segbudget}).

\paragraph{Between Backbones} To see whether one backbone was better for every method, Fig.~\ref{fig:bbgap} gives SPECTRE minus TAP-CT per method on the same labels or structures, with per-task and joint models pooled. In classification, SPECTRE was ahead with LoRA on \numBbdiffLoraSpectreAheadN{} of \numBbdiffLoraCohortsN{} cohorts and with the linear probe on \numBbdiffLinSpectreAheadN{}. As Dice is computed on each backbone's own grid and SPECTRE saw several of the segmentation datasets during pretraining (Tab.~\ref{tab:overlap}), we do not use these differences to rank the backbones.

\paragraph{Prediction Spread} To detect runs that did not train, we computed, for each backbone and method, the median over label--mode pairs (per-task and joint models) of the standard deviation of the held-out probabilities (Tab.~\ref{tab:spread}; rule in Appendix~\ref{app:stats}). Only SPECTRE Full FT fell below \numSpreadMinValue{} (\numSpreadSpectreFullft{}), whereas every other method stayed at or above \numSpreadOthermin{} on every backbone.

\section{Success and failure cases}
\label{app:cases}

The cases in Fig.~\ref{fig:casescls} (classification) and Figs.~\ref{fig:casesseg_amos22}, \ref{fig:casesseg_totalseg}, \ref{fig:casesseg_kits23}, \ref{fig:casesseg_lits} and~\ref{fig:casesseg_msd_pancreas} (segmentation) were chosen by a fixed rule from the held-out per-case results. For segmentation, each held-out case was scored by its Dice averaged over structures and over the joint models of Lin, Dec, LoRA and Full FT on TAP-CT and SPECTRE, where the AMOS22 and TotalSegmentator models are those trained for 24\,000 steps. Among the cases scored by all eight models, we show the case at the 90th percentile of this score as the success case and the lowest-scoring case as the failure case. For classification, we used, per source, the label with the most held-out positives, excluding the negative group of FDG-PET/CT. Each held-out positive was scored by the share of held-out negatives it outranks, averaged over the per-task models of Lin, LoRA, Attn-only FT and Full FT on every backbone evaluated on that cohort. The success case is the positive at the 90th percentile of this score, and the weakest positive is the one with the lowest score. For LiTS, Fig.~\ref{fig:casesseg_lits} instead shows one representative case with a reference tumor, chosen by the rule of Fig.~\ref{fig:examplesseg}.

\section{Extended discussion}
\label{app:discussion}
This appendix extends Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

Attention-only fine-tuning and LoRA cost about the same per training step because both back-propagate through every encoder block to reach the adapted weights of the first block (Tab.~\ref{tab:cost}). Our rankings also depend on the training budget (Sections~\ref{sec:clsres} and~\ref{sec:segres}), and the joint segmentation models were limited in part by their budget per structure.

Our study has several limitations. Each method was trained with a single default setting, with all adapters of rank 16 on the query, key and value projections, and only full fine-tuning was re-tuned after it failed; consequently, the ranking among the stronger methods may change with method-specific tuning, and seeds were repeated only for joint models. Future work could tune each method on selection data under a matched budget. Per-label tests also have limited power: LoRA was significantly ahead of the linear probe on none of the labels with fewer than 100 held-out cases and on \numSigbysizeLargeSig\% of those with 400 or more, although all of the latter come from RSNA19 ICH. Segmentation models had not converged at our training budgets and were scored on each backbone's working grid, so our results rank adaptation methods rather than measure their distance to nnU-Net \citep{isensee2021nnunet} or to models pretrained with segmentation labels \citep{li2024suprem,he2024vista3d}. We also report overlap (Dice) only: Dice does not measure boundary accuracy, and on a grid coarser than the image, such as the 224 in-plane grid of TAP-CT, it penalizes small structures most. Future work could compare adaptation methods at convergence with boundary metrics and evaluate structure-balanced crop sampling and a Dice computed over the batch for joint segmentation models. Lastly, two caveats concern the data: SPECTRE saw AMOS, LUNA25, LiTS, MSD Pancreas and most held-out KiTS23 cases, without labels, during self-supervised pretraining (Tab.~\ref{tab:overlap}), which may favor it on these datasets, and the AMOS release gives no patient identifiers, so patient-level separation of the AMOS part of the CVPR26 cohort cannot be confirmed (Tab.~\ref{tab:labelcounts}).
